Produce a scientific-method-focused whole-study review using the fixed rubric. Concentrate on the major claims, causal interpretation, hypotheses, strength of evidence, triangulation, orthogonal validation and consequential missing validation. Do not produce an exhaustive descriptive inventory of every panel.

The inputs include a complete source inventory, coverage metadata, original methods text where selected, and provisional page evidence records. Some original text may be omitted for budget reasons; omissions are enumerated. The page records are model-generated and can be mistaken. You are not seeing all original images anew in this synthesis. Resolve conflicts against original text and inventory where available; retain uncertainty if resolution is impossible.

Earlier page-local context_limits are not global absence. Do not discard a supplied method because an earlier record overlooked it. Source duplication and repeated summaries are not new independent evidence. If genuine whole-study absence cannot be established from the reviewed sources, classify the gap as unclear or local_context_only and explain the limit. Do not recommend an experiment already reported without explaining the remaining unresolved question.

Use up to eight major claims. Apply dimensions 11 and 12: where a claim depends on a comparison across many observations from few samples, on a comparison whose scale or model looks inappropriate, on hand-set thresholds, on batch-confounded groups, or on something not being detected, say so in the assessment and reflect it in the evidence rank. For each, separate the authors' claim from your assessment and identify causal scope, necessity/sufficiency, supporting experiments, alternatives, evidence rank and rationale. Explicitly evaluate triangulation and shared dependencies. Prioritize at most eight validation gaps by scientific consequence, and give discriminating experiments with competing predictions rather than generic calls for more data. Minor formatting/citation defects are not central unless they affect the science. Do not issue an automatic acceptance/rejection decision or a universal numeric quality score.


In addition, complete two sections. First, claim_measurement_links: for each major claim, name the entity or process the claim concerns, list what each supporting assay measures, and judge whether the link between those measurements and the claimed entity is directly_shown, inferred, not_shown or unclear. Do not accept agreement between different assays as proof that they report on the same entity. Second, reporting_and_statistics: up to ten findings on experimental units, replication, error representation, statistical tests, multiple testing, significance wording, software and parameters, data availability, unit of inference, scale and model fit, batch and sample metadata, negative evidence, and internal consistency of numbers, thresholds and statements between Methods, legends and Results. Base each finding on the original methods and legends and on the page records' statistics_reported fields. Do not report an item as missing unless the supplied methods and legends were checked; use not_found_in_reviewed_material rather than absent, and rank by consequence for the central claims.

Use no double quotation marks inside text values; quote manuscript wording with single quotation marks or paraphrase it, and finish every sentence.

A cross-check stage has already compared passages of the original text. Its findings are supplied with a quote_verified marker per quoted passage; the marker comes from code that searched the cited original block for the quote. Use verified conflicts as findings: reflect them under internal_consistency in reporting_and_statistics, and in the assessment of any major claim whose evidence they affect. A finding with an unverified quote is not confirmed; check it against the original text and methods, or report it as unclear. Do not add contradictions that neither the cross-check nor the original text supports, and do not treat a possible_conflict or ambiguity as a conflict.


An experiment inventory built from the original text is supplied, with quote_verified markers set by code. Use it as the description of what each experiment was, how it was replicated and which controls the text mentions. Rate each claim against the experiments that serve it. Where the inventory shows that an experiment counts lower-level observations as N, lacks a stated N, shows an example without stated repeats, or lacks controls that its design normally needs, say so in the assessment of the claim and in reporting_and_statistics; phrase missing controls as not mentioned in the supplied text. An inventory entry whose evidence quotes are unverified is not confirmed. The inventory does not replace the page records for what the figures show.


Per-experiment checks have also been run, one call per inventory entry against standards of good experimental practice, and are supplied with quote_verified markers. Use the concerns that have verified quotes as findings: carry each one that affects a major claim into that claim's assessment and evidence rank, and into reporting_and_statistics (unit_of_inference, scale_and_model, batch_and_metadata, negative_evidence, error_representation, statistical_tests and the other aspects as they fit). Weigh them by consequence for the central claims; a concern about a minor experiment does not change a claim that other experiments support independently. A concern whose quotes are unverified is not confirmed; check the original text or report it as unclear. Where checks mark an item not_assessable, say what the supplied text lacks. Do not invent concerns the checks and the original text do not support.


A knowledge graph of the experiments, produced by an independent extraction from the same manuscript, is supplied. It is descriptive and may contain errors: it can merge or split records, miss external datasets and resources, and leave fields not_reported that the text does state. Use it to see which data each analysis rests on, which experiments produce which datasets, which replicate and control information the extraction found, and where the extraction flagged contradictions or open questions. Check its statements against the original text before relying on them, do not treat its flags as findings by themselves, and do not cite it as a source.
